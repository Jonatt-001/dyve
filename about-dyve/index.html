01 / ENTITY

What is Dyve?

Dyve is the core identity associated with the Dyve HackaX intelligence platform. HackaX is the cybersecurity-focused publication and intelligence environment users encounter at dyve.online.

The Dyve identity

A system for following the signal, not just the headline.

Dyve is designed around a simple information problem: cybersecurity events rarely exist in isolation. A breach can connect to a vulnerability. A vulnerability can become part of an attack campaign. A campaign can involve a known threat actor, ransomware operation or underground ecosystem.

Dyve HackaX organizes those developments into recognizable intelligence areas so readers can move from what happened to why it matters and then to the wider context.

Entity relationship

Dyve, HackaX and Dyve Intelligence

Dyve is the primary identity. Dyve HackaX is the cybersecurity intelligence publication and interface. Dyve Intelligence describes the intelligence concept and systems presented through the platform.

These names represent connected parts of the same Dyve identity rather than unrelated cybersecurity brands.

05 / EDITORIAL MODEL

Reporting, intelligence and analysis

Dyve HackaX is designed to keep different forms of cybersecurity information understandable. A headline is not automatically an intelligence assessment, and a developing claim is not automatically a confirmed fact.

06 / TRUST MODEL

How Dyve handles intelligence

Cybersecurity information can change quickly. Dyve HackaX is structured to distinguish known information from developing claims and to provide context without presenting uncertainty as certainty.

01 / Attribution

Identify the information source

Reporting should make the origin of important claims and material information understandable whenever source attribution is available and appropriate.

02 / Verification

Separate fact from allegation

Developing reports, allegations and unconfirmed claims should not automatically be represented as established facts.

03 / Context

Explain why an event matters

Relevant incidents can be connected to vulnerabilities, threat actors, campaigns, technologies and wider trends.

04 / Corrections

Update when information changes

Cybersecurity reporting can evolve as new evidence appears. Corrections and updates are part of maintaining a useful record.

07 / Mission

Make the threat landscape legible.

Cybersecurity produces an enormous volume of headlines, indicators, disclosures, technical findings and competing claims. The difficult part is often not finding information; it is understanding how the pieces relate.

Dyve HackaX is built around that problem. Its purpose is to create a structured place where cybersecurity developments can be discovered, grouped by intelligence area, read in context and followed over time.
