Threat Intelligence Guide

What Are Cyber Threat Signals?

Understanding the early indicators that can reveal developing cyber threats before they become confirmed incidents.

Cyber threat signals are observable indicators, changes or anomalies that may provide evidence of developing malicious activity. They can include newly exploited vulnerabilities, suspicious domains, malware detections, malicious URLs, indicators of compromise and other changes in the cyber threat landscape.

A threat signal is not necessarily proof that an organization has been breached. Instead, it provides an early intelligence point that can be investigated, correlated with other observations and evaluated against additional evidence.

Dyve HackaX aggregates selected public cybersecurity intelligence observations and normalizes them into a common signal model. This makes it possible to examine different categories of activity within one intelligence layer while retaining the originating source for each observation.

Intelligence Methodology

How HackaX interprets a signal

A signal is an observable intelligence event. HackaX preserves the originating source, normalizes its data and applies a simple presentation-level priority model. Priority is not a substitute for vendor severity, incident response, attribution or independent validation.

Observation A source reports a vulnerability, IOC, malware detection or malicious infrastructure event.
Normalization Source-specific fields are converted into a consistent HackaX signal schema.
Assessment Recency, source context and available confidence data are used to present a priority level.
