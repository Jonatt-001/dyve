Nigerian Army X Account Targeted in Cyberattack, Suspicious Posts Trigger Warning


The Nigerian Army has raised an alarm over an attempted cyber breach of its official X account, warning Nigerians and the media to disregard suspicious or unauthorised information published through the platform.

The incident occurred at about 5:00 p.m. on Saturday, August 29, 2026, according to the Army, which said it immediately activated technical and protective measures to secure the account and prevent further unauthorised access.

In a statement signed by the Acting Director, Army Public Relations, Colonel Appolonia Anele, the military said the incident was being treated as a cybersecurity breach and that efforts were underway to restore exclusive administrative control of the account.

“The Nigerian Army has detected and is actively responding to an attempted unauthorised access to its official X (formerly Twitter) account.”

The Army said the measures being taken were intended not only to secure the account but also to protect the integrity of information distributed through the platform.

Suspicious posts appeared during the incident

The warning followed reports that the Army’s X account began publishing unusual cryptocurrency- and memecoin-related content on Saturday afternoon.

TheCable reported that the account started posting memecoin updates at around 3:50 p.m., while other reports also observed unusual activity from the military handle. The Army, however, has not publicly identified the individuals or group responsible for the attempted breach, nor has it disclosed the precise method used to gain or attempt to gain access. (thetimes.com.ng, thecable.ng)

That distinction is significant.

While some reports have characterised the incident as an outright hack, the Army’s official wording remains that there was an attempted unauthorised access and that the incident is being handled as a cybersecurity matter.

The military therefore advised the public not to automatically treat material appearing on the affected account as genuine Army communication while the security incident remains unresolved.

Army warns Nigerians against relying on suspicious posts

The Army urged members of the public, journalists and other stakeholders to exercise caution and disregard any suspicious, unauthorised, misleading or unverified content emanating from the affected account.

According to the military, such material should not be interpreted as representing its official position.

The warning is particularly important because the Nigerian Army uses social media as one of its channels for communicating information about military operations, security developments, public statements and other matters involving national security.

A compromised or improperly controlled account can therefore create a misinformation problem beyond the immediate loss of access.

An attacker controlling an official military account could potentially use the platform’s established credibility and large audience to distribute false operational information, impersonate military authorities, manipulate public perception or direct users toward malicious links.

There is currently no public evidence that the incident resulted in the disclosure of classified military information, and the Army has not said that operational systems or classified networks were compromised.

Incident comes shortly after DHQ X account compromise

The development is particularly notable because it comes roughly two weeks after the Defence Headquarters (DHQ) experienced a separate compromise of its official X account.

On August 15, the Defence Headquarters announced that its account had been compromised and warned the public to disregard suspicious or misleading content posted from the handle.

At the time, Premium Times reported that the compromised DHQ account had been flooded with cryptocurrency-related posts. The account, which had more than 980,000 followers, was used as one of the military’s public communication channels. (premiumtimesng.com)

The DHQ subsequently recovered the account. On August 18, the military confirmed its return with a brief message stating that it was “back” and thanking followers for their patience. (punchng.com)

The Nigerian Army incident therefore represents the second publicly reported cybersecurity incident involving a major Nigerian military X account within roughly two weeks.

That proximity is likely to increase scrutiny of how military and other government institutions protect their social-media credentials, administrator accounts and connected third-party applications.

Why the incidents matter

Official social-media accounts have increasingly become part of the communications infrastructure of governments, militaries and other public institutions.

For security agencies, the stakes are considerably higher.

A verified or widely recognised military account carries an inherent level of credibility. If attackers gain control, even temporarily, they may be able to exploit that credibility before administrators regain access.

The danger is not limited to embarrassing posts or cryptocurrency scams.

A malicious actor could potentially attempt to publish fabricated security alerts, false claims about military operations, fake recruitment announcements, manipulated statements or links designed to steal credentials from followers.

This is why cybersecurity specialists generally treat social-media accounts belonging to government and critical organisations as organisational assets rather than ordinary personal profiles.

The US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA), for example, recommends strong authentication, careful control of third-party applications, protection of API credentials and immediate replacement of compromised credentials when there is evidence that account secrets may have been exposed. It also recommends stronger forms of multi-factor authentication, including authenticator applications and physical security keys where appropriate. (cisa.gov)

Army provides alternative channels for verification

Pending the full resolution of the incident, the Nigerian Army directed members of the public and the media to rely on its other official communication channels.

The Army listed the following platforms:

  • Facebook: @hqnigerianarmy and @nigerianarmyinfo
  • Instagram: @hqnigerianarmy and @nigerianarmyinfo
  • X: @nigerianarmyinfo
  • YouTube: @hqnigerianarmy and @nigerianarmyinfotv

The military said these channels should be used to verify information while efforts to secure the affected account continue.

The Army also thanked the public for its vigilance and cooperation, saying further updates would be provided as necessary. (tvcnews.tv)

What remains unclear

The Army has not publicly disclosed:

  • who attempted to access the account;
  • the identity or location of the suspected attackers;
  • how access was obtained or attempted;
  • whether administrator credentials were compromised;
  • whether any third-party application or connected service was involved;
  • whether sensitive information was accessed;
  • or whether the affected X account has been fully recovered.

Those details will be important in determining whether the incident was primarily an account-level compromise, a credential attack, a phishing-related intrusion, abuse of a connected application or another form of social-engineering attack.

For now, the military’s public position is that an unauthorised access attempt occurred and that protective measures have been activated.

A growing cybersecurity warning for official accounts

The two incidents involving the DHQ and Nigerian Army within a short period highlight a broader problem facing organisations that depend heavily on social platforms for public communication.

The security of an official account ultimately depends on more than the password used to access it. Administrator privileges, authentication methods, recovery channels, connected applications, access tokens and the security practices of everyone with administrative access can all become part of an organisation’s attack surface.

For the public, the immediate lesson is simpler: an official-looking social-media post should not automatically be treated as authentic during a reported account compromise.

Nigerians should verify sensitive claims through another official channel before sharing or acting on them, particularly where the information concerns military operations, national security, emergency instructions, financial schemes or requests for personal information.

The Nigerian Army said it remains committed to information integrity, operational security and responsible strategic communication as efforts to secure the affected account continue.
