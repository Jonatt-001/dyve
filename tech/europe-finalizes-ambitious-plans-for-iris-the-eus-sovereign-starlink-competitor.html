Europe Finalizes Ambitious Plans for IRIS²: The EU’s ‘Sovereign’ Starlink Competitor

By Dyve Tech Intelligence Unit • August 7, 2026 • 3 min read

Are we about to witness a new space race for global internet supremacy? The European Union has officially moved past the planning stage for IRIS², its highly anticipated low Earth orbit (LEO) satellite broadband network.

More from Dyve Tech →

SpaceX’s Starlink has long dominated the skies with its massive satellite constellation, but Europe is officially gearing up to secure its own digital sovereignty. After months of intense negotiations, the European Commission, the European Space Agency (ESA), and key European aerospace manufacturers have finalized the implementation plan for IRIS²—a cutting-edge satellite internet network designed to provide highly secure and reliable broadband services across the EU and beyond.

Here is a detailed breakdown of what Europe’s new satellite mega-project entails, how it compares to Starlink, and what it means for the future of global connectivity.

Moving From Blueprint to Orbit

Negotiations for the IRIS² project kicked off in January, and the recent agreement marks a monumental shift from theoretical planning to full-scale manufacturing and deployment.

The finalized plans lock in the detailed design of the satellite constellation, outline the construction protocols, and secure the necessary ground infrastructure and launch services. Furthermore, the European Commission has successfully finalized the project's target pricing and locked in crucial investments from private operators, ensuring the financial stability of the mission.

A Bigger, Bolder Constellation: 348 Satellites

In a major update to the original blueprint, European manufacturing partners have agreed to construct and deliver significantly more satellites than initially planned.

The IRIS² constellation will now feature a total of 348 satellites in low Earth orbit, an addition of 66 satellites over the original target. According to the ESA, this expansion is not just about broader coverage; it is a strategic move to heavily fortify capabilities for defense, security, and emergency services.

What do these extra 66 satellites achieve?

• Boosted Governmental Capacity: Secure communications capacity for EU governments will increase by 60%.

• Global Reach Expansion: Global secure capacity will jump by 54%.

• Crisis Management: It significantly enhances Europe’s ability to support critical, high-stakes missions during global crises.

IRIS² vs. Starlink: Quality and Security Over Quantity

When you compare the numbers, IRIS² looks minuscule next to Elon Musk’s SpaceX, which currently operates a massive web of over 10,000 active Starlink satellites. However, the two networks are built with entirely different primary objectives.

Starlink was conceived as a commercial broadband network aimed at mass consumer adoption. IRIS², on the other hand, prioritizes digital sovereignty and ultra-secure communications for government entities.

The primary users of IRIS² will be the governments of the European Union, alongside participating nations like Norway and Iceland. The network is explicitly designed to be an unbreakable communications backbone during critical operations, such as:

• Emergency and disaster response.

• Coordination of humanitarian aid.

• High-level border surveillance and national security operations.

Will Regular Citizens Get to Use IRIS²?

While the initial focus is strictly on government and military applications, the ultimate vision for IRIS² includes commercial rollout.

In the future, the network will be opened up to private businesses and everyday citizens. It aims to bridge the digital divide by providing high-speed internet to rural, remote, or underserved areas where traditional terrestrial networks are either nonexistent or highly unreliable.

When Will IRIS² Launch?

The timeline is aggressive but achievable. The European Commission and its aerospace partners plan to launch the first batch of IRIS² satellites in 2029. Rather than waiting for the entire constellation to be in orbit, the EU intends to roll out its broadband services progressively starting that same year.

The Bottom Line

Europe’s decision to heavily invest in its own satellite network proves that reliance on foreign private entities for critical communications is no longer an option for the EU. While IRIS² won't outnumber Starlink in the sky, it represents a massive leap forward for European space independence, defense capabilities, and future-proof internet connectivity.
